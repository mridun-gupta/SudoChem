# ENERGY-008 — Real-World Case Studies and Application Lessons

**Evidence cutoff:** 03 November 2026  
**Use:** Engineering context and model/data-gap prioritisation. These cases do not validate ENERGY-008 calculations, equipment sizing, economics or safety.

## Case 1 — Kassø, Denmark: commercial e-methanol from renewable electricity and biogenic CO₂

European Energy's Kassø facility was inaugurated in May 2025 and began supplying e-methanol. Public project sources report a nominal capacity of 42,000 t/y, approximately 52 MW electrolyser capacity, a nearby 304 MW solar park, and biogenic CO₂ sourced from the Tønder biogas facility. European Energy reported first raw e-methanol in March 2025 and industry-grade product above 99.85% purity in April 2025. Offtakers include Maersk, LEGO and Novo Nordisk. The plant's output is used in shipping and chemical/materials applications.

**What this demonstrates:** a real industrial configuration for Route C; coupling electrolyser operation to renewable power; local biogenic CO₂ logistics; multi-line methanol synthesis; product-quality commissioning; and demand-side offtake across fuel and chemical applications.

**ENERGY-008 application:** use as a process-architecture and interface checklist (renewable power/electrolyser, H₂ conditioning, CO₂ conditioning, synthesis loop, product purification, storage/offtake, certification). It supports the plausibility of Route C as a candidate, not the project's 50 t/day basis, assumed operating pressure, energy KPIs, emissions intensity or equipment dimensions. Public capacity figures are project-level disclosures and should not be reverse-engineered into vendor performance without a consistent boundary.

Sources: European Energy (12-Mar-2025; 03-Apr-2025; 13-May-2025); Hydrogen Council and McKinsey, *Global Hydrogen Compass 2025*; Danish Energy Export / EIFO project case.

## Case 2 — Piteå, Sweden: black-liquor gasification syngas to methanol/DME demonstration

Luleå University of Technology describes a demonstration project using synthesis gas produced from black-liquor gasification at the Chemrec facility in Piteå. The syngas contains CO, CO₂ and H₂, but also H₂S, methane and trace contaminants. The project explicitly identifies gas characterisation and removal of sulphur/tars as necessary to protect the Cu/ZnO methanol catalyst before demonstrating methanol/DME synthesis.

**What this demonstrates:** Route B is technically meaningful but cannot be represented by the same CO₂/H₂-only reaction ledger as Route C. Feedstock conversion, gas composition adjustment, contaminant removal and catalyst protection are material process sections.

**ENERGY-008 application:** Route B's evidence plan must include biomass/black-liquor assay, gasifier product composition and yield, tar/particulate/sulphur removal, syngas ratio adjustment, catalyst tolerance, and a separate utility/waste ledger. The Piteå project is a demonstration, not a full commercial biomass-to-methanol plant, and its black-liquor feed is not interchangeable with generic agricultural residues.

## Case 3 — Skive, Denmark: commercial biomass gasification with CHP (adjacent application)

The Skive plant is reported as a commercial biomass gasification deployment operating since 2008, producing syngas for combined heat and power rather than methanol. Public project descriptions report approximately 12 MWth district heat and 6 MWe from wood pellets using bubbling fluidized-bed gasification and catalytic reforming.

**What this demonstrates:** biomass handling, gasification, gas cleanup/reforming and integration with useful heat/electricity are industrially deployed, but product synthesis and methanol catalyst performance are not demonstrated by this case.

**ENERGY-008 application:** use only as an upstream gasification/energy-integration analogue for Route B. Do not cite it as evidence of commercial biomass-to-methanol yield, methanol quality or methanol plant economics.

## Case-study implications for the route and design gates

| Design question | Case evidence | ENERGY-008 action |
|---|---|---|
| Can Route C be implemented commercially? | Kassø provides a commercial e-methanol precedent | Retain Route C as provisional candidate; obtain site-specific biogenic CO₂ and renewable power contracts |
| Is product quality merely stoichiometric? | Kassø reported commissioning product above 99.85% purity | Add validated purification and product-specification evidence; do not infer product quality from ideal reactor ledger |
| Can Route B use a simple CO₂/H₂ model? | Piteå syngas includes CO, CO₂, H₂ and catalyst poisons | Keep Route B blocked until syngas composition, cleanup and catalyst data exist |
| Does gasification evidence prove methanol economics? | Skive demonstrates biomass gasification CHP, not methanol synthesis | Use only for upstream unit-operation analogy; separate methanol synthesis costs and yields |
| Can case capacity be scaled directly? | Public project figures use different boundaries and configurations | No direct scale-up; establish a consistent basis, utility boundary, availability and vendor data |
